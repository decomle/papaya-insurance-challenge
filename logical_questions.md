# Q1 - My first memory

My first memory is a trip to the North of Vietnam when I was 12. Back then, my father was a bus driver. During a summer holiday, he took me to Thai Binh—his hometown. I got to experience the beauty of Vietnamese nature, from high mountains to white sand beaches and endless rice fields.

I don't really remember much about Thai Binh, but I do remember that it was a poor, underdeveloped province with lots of rice fields. I got to play in the rice fields for the first time in my life, and we flew a traditional flute kite. In the sky, the kite made a soothing sound—at least to me, though it might be annoying to someone else.

I'm not good at remembering things from the past, but for some reason, this memory has stuck with me.

# Q3 - Do you give names to things you own? List a few of them

No, I find naming inanimate objects a bit cheesy and childish, to be honest. The only exception would be something sentimental, like a gift from my parents, my wife, or my children. Those things have emotional value, so I might give them a special name. For example:
- "ngựa già" - aka "old horse": A gift from my mom when I get my first job
- "Vợ 2" - aka "second wife": My old car - the first expensive thing that I bought

# Q5 - The last time I forget my thing

I took a trip to Ke Ga, a remote and beautiful town with white sand beaches in Binh Thuan province. . It is about 200 km from my home and takes almost four hours to get there. It was a long drive, so we stopped by a café to take a rest, and I left my key there. When we reached our destination and parked the car, I realized that I had forgotten my key at the café. After a moment of panic, I called my mom for help. I asked her to go to my house and send my spare key to Phan Thiet, a nearby city. Luckily, the spare key arrived the next day. I left my wife and kids at the resort and took a bus to the city to pick it up, getting our trip back on track.

After this incident, I attached my car key to a carabiner clip. Whenever I leave the car, I hook it to my pants, and I have almost never forgotten it since. Also, when we got back, I traced our route and noted down everything I could remember about where we stopped, including a nearby gas station. On our next road trip, believe it or not, I was able to recover the lost key.

# Q6 - Best position in the Cinema Queue

Probably the first third of the queue. Why? Because you won't have to wait too long to get inside, and you still get to watch the trailers. However, the front of the line can sometimes feel cramped and crowded. So, if I prefer a bit of quiet, I'd rather stay toward the back of the queue.

# Q8 - How to fill up the gas tank

I'm not sure how people do this in other countries, but in Vietnam, it's very straightforward. First, look up the nearest gas station on Google Maps. Make sure the station has the fuel compatible with your vehicle. Drive your vehicle there, or if you're completely out of fuel, bring a container.

Ask the attendant to fill it up for you and pay for the fuel. That's it—you don't have to pump it yourself or worry about price differences between stations, as fuel prices are regulated and fixed here.
